About

I am a Ph.D. candidate in Computer Science at Texas Tech University, working on quantum machine learning under a Distinguished Graduate Student Assistantship. I am also, and just as deliberately, a mentor to Vietnamese students who want to study abroad.

I did not take the fast route. I finished my engineering degree at HUST in 2004, spent twelve years building software at FPT Software, taught for six years at FPT University, and only then went abroad — first to France on an Erasmus Mundus joint programme, then to the United States on a funded Ph.D. assistantship. I started my doctorate at an age when most people have decided that door has closed. It had not.

That is the reason this page exists. I know the Vietnamese application pipeline from the inside — as an applicant who was rejected before he was accepted, as a lecturer who wrote hundreds of recommendation letters, and now as a researcher who sits on the other side of the table, reviewing for Pattern Recognition and Neural Networks and reading student work every semester.

Study-Abroad Mentoring

I work with a small number of Vietnamese students each cycle, mainly those applying to funded master's and Ph.D. programmes in computer science, engineering and related fields in the United States and Europe. I am not an agency and I do not take a commission from any university.

What I help with

Finding the right programmes

Building a realistic list — reach, match and safety — based on your actual profile rather than rankings. Identifying which programmes are funded and which quietly are not.

Contacting professors

How to read a paper well enough to write a cold email that gets answered, and how to follow up without becoming noise. This is the single highest-leverage step for funded Ph.D. admission.

Statement of purpose & CV

Structuring a research narrative that a committee can follow. I read what you wrote and tell you what an admissions reader will actually think — not what you hope they will think.

Recommendation letters

Choosing referees, briefing them properly, and what a strong letter contains. I have written many from the lecturer's side and read many from the reviewer's side.

Scholarships & assistantships

Erasmus Mundus, university assistantships, departmental fellowships, and the timing that decides whether you are considered for them at all.

Research experience before you apply

How to get a first publication or a real project on your CV while still in Vietnam. Several of my co-authors were undergraduates when we started.

Why my advice is worth taking — and where it is not

I have been through the process as a Vietnamese applicant twice, to two different systems, at an age that made both harder. I teach at a U.S. department and see admitted students' files and their first two years. I review for two Q1 Elsevier journals and four international conferences, so I know what "research potential" reads like on paper.

What I am not: an immigration lawyer, a visa service, or an expert on undergraduate admissions, the UK, Australia, or non-technical fields. If your question is in one of those areas I will say so and point you elsewhere rather than guess.
